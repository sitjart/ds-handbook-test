## Why this case

Before 2020, research data management (RDM) support across Finnish higher education institutions (HEIs) and research-performing organisations (RPOs) was fragmented. While national Open Science initiatives (2011 onward) established and funded by the Ministry of Education and Culture had successfully created policy frameworks and expert groups, there was no low-threshold, informal space for data support professionals to connect, exchange practical knowledge, and seek peer support.

Those affected were primarily data stewards, data support staff, and IT specialists working closely with researchers. These professionals often operated in isolation within their own institutions, despite facing very similar day-to-day challenges. Researchers were indirectly affected through uneven support quality and lack of shared practices across institutions, while leadership lacked a clear channel into grassroots RDM issues.

Finland already had strong national coordination in Open Science, and collaboration structures were in place, but they were largely formal and task-driven. There was a clear gap for an interest-based community. The COVID-19 pandemic also accelerated the shift toward virtual collaboration, making a national, low-barrier network both feasible and necessary. This combination of mature policy infrastructure and unmet practical needs created the opportunity to establish the [CSC Data Support Network](https://wiki.eduuni.fi/spaces/csckorkeakoulut/pages/158139607/CSC+-+Data+Support+Network).



## What we did

### The approach

The CSC Data Support Network was launched in August 2020 as a lightweight, inclusive community built on existing national Open Science structures rather than replacing them. The idea was to complement formal working groups with an informal, practitioner-driven network. 

The network is a part of the [CSC Research Data Management Competence Center](https://csc.fi/en/our-expertise/research-data-management/), which promotes and supports the skills and competence development of research data management and aims to foster Open Science and FAIR data management. CSC - IT Center for Science itself is owned by the state and Finnish higher education institutions (HEIs). CSC provides comprehensive scientific computing, data management, and analysis services and solutions for researchers in every discipline, as well as training to use our services. CSC also hosts the Finnish ELIXIR Node.

__First move:__ CSC organized an initial kick-off event and leveraged existing Open Science coordination channels to reach potential members. Because the community built on already well-connected expert groups, outreach was efficient and adoption relatively fast.

__Tools and formats used:__

* A moderated mailing list for monthly newsletters
* A discussion forum (“data chat”) requiring a Finnish eDuuni-ID
* Monthly virtual “data coffee” meetings via Zoom
* Training events and open materials (CC BY 4.0 licensed)

The design principle throughout was low threshold participation: minimal commitments, easy access, and no additional workload expectations.

__Engagement in practice:__

* Monthly newsletters shared curated updates on RDM, training, and services
* Open discussion forum enabled peer-to-peer problem solving
* Informal monthly meetings allowed free discussion, occasionally with short presentations
* Training events were co-created with network experts, increasing ownership

This was not built from scratch. It was explicitly built on:

* National Open Science coordination structures (expert groups and working groups)
* CSC’s existing role in delivering RDM services and training
* Established communities around ELIXIR and other infrastructures

__Effort involved:__ The network is coordinated centrally (within CSC), but designed to scale through community contributions. While no exact FTE is defined, the model relied on light but continuous coordination rather than large, time-bound projects.

__Iteration and pivot:__ Initially, community engagement included in-person café meetings, but due to COVID-19 these were rapidly replaced with virtual meetings. This shift proved highly successful, significantly increasing inclusiveness and participation nationwide.

## What we'd tell others (mandatory)

The lesson, the caveats, the conditions for replication, and what
happens next – bundled because they overlap in practice.

- **What worked, and why.** The one or two things you'd repeat without
  hesitation if you were starting over.
- **What didn't work, and how you adapted.** The corner you painted
  yourself into. Future contributors learn most from these.
- **Transferability and scaling potential.** Can this approach be reused
  by another institution or individual? What conditions are required
  (leadership buy-in, existing service, specific funding, a particular
  legal context)? What adaptations might be needed? Be honest about
  what was special about your setting.
- **Sustainability and next steps.** How is the work currently supported
  or funded? Who maintains or owns it? What are the plans for
  continuation, embedding, or scaling? If continuity is uncertain, say
  so.
